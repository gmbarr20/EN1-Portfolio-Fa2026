Project 10: Haunted House

Phase 1: Spider Mock-up and Rigging

Phase 2: Painting and Finalization

Question

The task for this project was to create an installation for the haunted house in the Nolop Workshop. We were tasked to use any or all of the LEGO Spike Prime skills from past projects, as well as take advantage of any of the crafting equipment from the class or in workshops around campus. There were little restraints on the size of the project, so my partner, Rain Chen, and I went big.

Process

The process was split into two main phases. The first phase was rough construction of the spider on my part and breadboarding by Rain for the lights in the spider's eyes. Once the spider body had been drafted, I made a wooden base using spare 2-by-4s from the Nolop Red Zone to suspend the spider in midair. Once the spider was up, I started on the winch system to raise and lower the legs. None of the parts in our Spike Prime kit could hold the fishing line steady, so we 3D-printed some spools and attached them to the motors. The initial tests of the moving legs can be seen in the Phase 1 videos. From this point, Rain and I started to get an idea of the size and presence of our project.

Phase 2 revolved around painting, putting the electronics and cardboard construction together, and finalizing the winch system. We first spray-painted the stand all black, as well as added a black base coat to the spider. Then, to make the spider more visible in the dark, we added silver and purple colors to the joints. We then attached the four motors and fishing line to the front four legs and tested to make sure they all moved asymmetrically. We then cut a hole in the front of the body to place the breadboard and electrical systems inside the spider. Because Rain had attached the eight LED eyes to the face, we could then place the face on the front and cover the hole. We then tested to make sure everything could work for an hour and waited for the haunted house doors to open.

Result

The result was the project you can see in the bottom-most video. The spider had four fully motor-controlled robotic legs and eight blinking red LED eyes, all while being suspended in the air. The spider drew many eyes in the haunted house and caused a great fright in most of the attendees. The spider also drew the attention of the dean of the Tufts University School of Engineering, who stopped to take a video. Rain and I were incredibly satisfied with the final product and hope to make even bigger and better projects in the future.
