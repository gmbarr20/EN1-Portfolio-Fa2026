Project 7: Draw a Square

Question

Our task here was to create a robot with LEGO Spike Prime that could autonomously draw a square. This project was building off of the Project 6: Simple Car techniques, and required fine motor control and orientation code. The final part of this project was a one-opportunity test during class to draw a square, with the challenge being that the robot had to be reliable and consistent enough to perform when needed.

Process

The process began with designing the double-marker frame at the front of the robot so it could draw two lines at once. Two motors, one for each wheel, provided driving control and allowed the robot to turn in place. A third point of contact, using a ball bearing, was added for stability. A tilt motor was added to the marker frame because initial testing showed that the markers would draw over each other when the robot turned 90 degrees to draw the next two lines of the square. With the tilt motor, the robot could lift the markers, reorient itself, and place them back down to continue drawing. One issue visible in the video is that the driving-wheel motor speed was initially set too low, causing choppy motion and zigzagging lines. After increasing the wheel speed in the code, the lines became significantly smoother in the final test. On test day, the robot was plugged in and started, and the rest of the task happened fully autonomously.

Result

The final result is the robot seen in the images and video above. It has two markers mounted to a tilt frame in the front, and two side-by-side wheels for driving. It was able to draw two lines at a time, and consistently drew a square within the boundaries of the laminated template.
